The person count clears the stated minimum. Places and associations fall short of theirs: some notices are absorbed into the entry above them when the first-line indent is lost to OCR, and some association notices are coded as `topic` because they carry none of the organisational template's markers. Treat the place and organisation tables as high-precision, incomplete samples of their populations, not as complete enumerations.

## 6. Known limitations

- **Segmentation.** Entry boundaries come from the first-line indent plus alphabetical order. Where the indent is lost, a notice is merged into the one above it; `n_paragraphs` flags the entries most at risk.
- **OCR.** The BnF estimates ~96% character accuracy for this volume. Proper names carry the errors that matter most here: surnames are the join key for the network, so a garbled surname becomes an isolated node.
- **Name resolution.** Matching is by surname, with a one-character tolerance flagged as `resolved_fuzzy`. Homonyms are left unresolved.
- **Coverage is Lambert's coverage.** Who appears in this book was decided in 1912 by a Tunis publisher: French settlers and officials are heavily over-represented, Tunisian Muslims and Jews appear mainly as notables, and women are almost absent. The dataset reproduces that selection exactly. It is evidence about the colonial elite's self-portrait, not a census.
